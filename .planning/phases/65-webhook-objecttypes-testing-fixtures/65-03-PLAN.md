  </decision>

  <context>
    Both precedents exist in this tree, and they disagree:

    - **`TaxId` was moved.** `lib/lattice_stripe/testing/fixtures/tax_id.ex` exists and
      `test/support/fixtures/tax_id.ex` does not. One canonical definition.
    - **`Dispute` was duplicated.** `test/support/fixtures/dispute.ex` and
      `lib/lattice_stripe/testing/fixtures/dispute.ex` both exist with byte-identical function
      bodies, and **nothing in the test suite locks them together** — silent drift by construction.
      65-PATTERNS.md § Anti-Precedent flags this explicitly as a shape not to copy.

    The ROADMAP mandates *move* for the entitlements fixture but is silent on these three. The cost
    of moving is five caller lines total, three of which are `import`s — and an `import` rename
    changes only the module path, leaving call sites untouched.

    The naming half: the public fixture surface is uniformly `*_json/1` across all ten existing
    modules. `Subscription` uses `basic/1`. Promoting it verbatim would introduce the project's
    first public `basic/1` and permanently break that convention. Renaming costs one external
    caller plus three internal call sites at `subscription.ex:54`, `:71`, and `:85`, where
    `with_items/1`, `paused/1`, and `canceled/1` each compose on the base.

    Note the Q1 decision in 65-02 deliberately did **not** rename the meter `basic/1` builders,
    because no requirement scoped that. If `subscription_json` is chosen here, the public surface
    will be mixed: `*_json/1` for core billing and entitlements, `basic/1` for meter fixtures.
    That is a real consequence to weigh, not a detail.
  </context>

  <options>
    <option id="move-and-rename">
      <name>Move, and rename to subscription_json/1 (RECOMMENDED — 65-RESEARCH.md § Q2)</name>
      <pros>
        Follows the `TaxId` precedent and leaves exactly one definition of each fixture, so drift
        is impossible rather than merely unlikely. Preserves the uniform public `*_json/1`
        convention for core billing. Five caller lines is genuinely cheap.
      </pros>
      <cons>
        Touches five caller files plus three internal call sites. Leaves the public surface mixed
        against the meter fixtures' `basic/1` unless a later phase reconciles them.
      </cons>
    </option>
    <option id="move-keep-basic">
      <name>Move, but keep basic/1</name>
      <pros>
        Same single-definition benefit with no rename churn; internally consistent with the meter
        fixtures promoted in 65-02.
      </pros>
      <cons>
        Ships the project's first public `basic/1` on a core-billing fixture, permanently breaking
        the ten-for-ten `*_json/1` convention adopters have learned.
      </cons>
    </option>
    <option id="duplicate">
      <name>Duplicate — leave the private copies in place</name>
      <pros>
        Zero caller updates; nothing existing can break.
      </pros>
      <cons>
        Reproduces the `Dispute` hazard three more times. Must then also ship a drift lock (an
        assertion that each public builder equals its private twin) or the copies silently diverge.
        Adds maintenance surface to avoid five one-line edits.
      </cons>
    </option>
  </options>

  <resume-signal>Select: move-and-rename, move-keep-basic, or duplicate</resume-signal>

  <reversibility rating="one-way">
    Public fixture module and function names become semver-covered on the Hex 1.8.0 tag. If
    `duplicate` is chosen, the drift-lock obligation is also permanent.
  </reversibility>
